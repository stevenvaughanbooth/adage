# Adage brand: adversarial review

## Verdict

The book is disciplined and mostly honest, but its two decided nouns describe the product backwards (a biological reflex is innate and bypasses the brain; an Adage "reflex" is learned and fed *into* the brain) and fight the railway world the visual identity owns. The proof is measured against the wrong baseline: a technical buyer's real alternative is a ten-minute CLAUDE.md, which the book never beats, only asserts past. Fix the baseline, then the metaphor, then the lexicon.

## Where it breaks

1. **Biology is inverted.** Reflexes are innate; learned ones are *conditioned*. The book claims "the distinction is biological" and "Reflexes skip the brain" while Voice says "the agent decides". A signal enters the model's context; it skips nothing. Fix: define reflex once as conditioned; delete the biology claim and "skip the brain"; lead with "Known routes skip the GPU".
2. **Wrong baseline.** "Nobody writes anything" and 2-vs-20 are measured against a blank agent; buyers will say a rules file does this in a paragraph. Fix: three-arm eval (no rules / hand-written CLAUDE.md / Adage) and a claim that survives it: "Nobody has to keep it true".
3. **Two metaphor systems.** Words are body (instinct, reflex, brain, flinch); marks are railway (rail, track, signal, lamp, aspect, signal box). The rewrite demotes the only line tying copy to marks. Fix: railway is the world, the nouns run on it; restore "Put your agent on rails" as tagline, the primitives as descriptor.
4. **Lexicon is over-built.** Twelve terms, three for one object (route/instinct/reflex), two for one property (lamp/aspect), under "One word per thing". "Signal" collides with Unix and JS signals; "aspect" with AOP. Fix: five outward words (instinct, reflex, track, signal, lamp); rest schema-only.
5. **The tagline names no category or benefit** and bills instincts (a few generic rules) equally with reflexes. Fix: never without "for coding agents" and a number nearby.
6. **Primitive identity mutates in transit.** A shared reflex "becomes your instinct"; nothing says what happens to its evidence, or to an instinct that gathers local evidence. Fix: one provenance field, one stated evidence rule.
7. **Visual identity is the 2026 default.** Near-black, violet, Instrument Serif with one italic word, a glowing line; three green-amber-red dots read as macOS window controls. Fix: make the reflex-arc glyph the mark, own an off-default hue, or own Day mode while competitors are dark.
8. **The "memory" ban erases the category** while the book says "forgets everything", "the CPU remembers". Fix: "Not memory. Behaviour, with evidence."
9. **No trust story for shared tracks.** `adage add` installs text injected into an agent: a prompt-injection surface. "Packs are signed" is offered as proof; only releases are. Fix: sign tracks or cut the claim.

## Trademark and naming exposure

- **AMD INSTINCT** (registered; 2024 filing covers class 9 software). Adage's story is GPU-versus-CPU with a rendered die; the goods are adjacent. Descriptive use holds only while it stays descriptive: `adage instincts`, a bespoke glyph and the headline "Instincts on day one" edge toward source identification.
- **Instinct (Spear Street, $2.5B)**: perception risk exceeds legal risk. "Reasoning is expensive. Instinct is free." reads as their slogan or a jab. Never singular, capitalised, abstract; always plural and possessed.
- **REFLEX (Pynecone, class 42)**, now an AI agent platform, in the class Adage will file. "Reflex layer" is a category label; category labels become marks. Retire it.
- **ADAGE** is a common word; expect co-existing registrations. The book says "file", not "cleared". Clear in 9 and 42; register the domain before the film ships.

## Claims that will get attacked

- **10×, 2 vs 20.** Attack: n=18, founder-built repo, Haiku 4.5 (the model most likely to wander), tasks the 54 training sessions already solved. Defense: model, count and repo in the same breath; add a stronger model and the CLAUDE.md arm.
- **−61% cost.** Attack: "$0.08 saved per task after $4.87 of training." Defense: publish the break-even (about 60 tasks) first.
- **100% hidden checks.** Attack: ceiling effect. Softening: 18/18 vs 14/18.
- **"$0 of inference", "replays on the CPU".** Attack: the model still reads and acts on the tokens; the CPU ran a regex. Softening: "the model reads a hint instead of rediscovering it".
- **"Never written by hand."** Attack: instincts are. Softening: scope to reflexes.
- **"No model." "Deterministic."** Attack: an optional embedding model exists; the hook is deterministic, the agent is not. Softening: "no model at decision time", "deterministic trigger".

- Lexicon retires "pack" except "instinct pack"; the book uses "curated packs", "shipped packs".
- Brief and schema say **yellow**; the book says **amber**.
- read-linked-files (rate 0.94) is amber; lookup-work-item (0.70) is green; nothing says what the lamp encodes.
- "Never set marketing copy in mono" vs closing chips and every label.
- "Every number has a denominator" vs tiles "100%", "−61%", "5 ms".

## Keep

- "Recorded, not staged", "Fail-open", "Text only. Never runs a thing": trust lines competitors cannot copy without shipping the property.
- The red lamp: dead ends with support and rate are the genuinely novel object.
- The discipline: a Retire column, a proof rule tied to `evals/datasets`, a Do/Don't card.
