How it works

From a transcript to a reflex, stage by stage.

Adage learns from what your agents already did. Nine stages take a raw session transcript to a one-line directive delivered at the right moment, and then check that it helped. Step through each one below, then read the math that decides every threshold.

          The math

          Every threshold, written down.

          Adage never asks a model whether a reflex is good. Each decision is arithmetic over counted traces, with the constants below. The values below are the defaults.

          Reading tracesEvidenceIntentGraph engineThe indexThe hookMeasurementTraining
          01

          Reading traces: one shape for every harness

          A transcript becomes a prompt followed by a flat list of steps. Tool calls that share one message id form one turn; results join their calls by id, wherever they appear. A compound shell command splits into one step per sub-action, and a failure is blamed on its last part only, unless the command used ||.

          Each step gets a template key, with specifics replaced (a ticket becomes {id}), and an effect. Effects are ranked, and the rank caps how strongly a step may ever be directed:

          1. observereads, lookups
          2. verifytests, checks
          3. regeneratecodegen, builds
          4. environmentinstalls
          5. authoredits
          6. publishpush, release

          Observe and verify steps may fire. A regenerate step fires only after its edit. An install primes at most and starts nascent until a person reviews it. Edits and publishing are never directed. Traces that Adage already steered are marked, and the miner for that kind of reflex skips them, so a reflex never learns from its own advice.

          In the code: crates/adage-learn/src/{lib,import,codex,opencode,cache}.rs · crates/adage-core/src/types.rs

          02

          Evidence: the Wilson lower bound

          Evidence is counted in distinct traces. Out of n traces where a reflex had its chance, k took its path. A rate from few traces is uncertain, so strength is judged by the lower end of the 95% Wilson score interval, not by the rate:

          LB= p^+z22n−zp^(1−p^)n+z24n2 1+z2n ,p^=kn,z=1.959964

          Try it: how many traces does a reflex need to fire?

          fire bar 0.80

          Fire
          LB ≥ 0.80 and k ≥ 5, within the effect's ceiling
          Prime
          a new reflex primes at a rate of 0.50; an existing one keeps its place down to 0.30
          Finish line
          the command must belong to the edit: P(c∣e)−P(c∣¬e)≥0.30
          Dead end
          the runner fails in ≥ 9 of 10 traces that ran it, a different check works within 5 steps, and the pair appears in ≥ 5 traces; dead ends always inhibit

          In the code: crates/adage-tracks/src/model.rs (wilson_lower) · crates/adage-learn/src/mine.rs

          03

          Intent: prompts that mean the same thing

          Prompts are cut to their first 128 words, with tickets, paths, URLs, SHAs, versions and code replaced by placeholders. A pinned 31 MB static model embeds each one as the mean of its WordPiece token rows, scaled to unit length, so similarity is a dot product:

          e(x)=v‖v‖,v=1|T|∑t∈TWt,cos(a,b)=e(a)·e(b)

          Each prompt links to its 10 nearest neighbours (found exactly, ties broken by index), and links below cosine 0.60 are cut. Louvain then groups the graph by modularity, visiting nodes in a fixed order so the result never varies:

          Q=12m∑i,j[Aij−γkikj2m]δ(ci,cj),γ=2.0

          A community becomes a family when at least 60% of its traces took the same first route (the first check or lookup before editing). Routes that appear in more than 80% of all traces say nothing about intent and are ignored. Each kept family gets its own threshold, chosen on this repository's prompts:

          t*=arg⁡maxtLB(kt,nt)

          Here nt counts prompts at cosine ≥ t from the family centre and kt those whose trace ran the family's route. At the hook, a new prompt matches the most similar centre whose threshold it clears; it fires only if that family's LB reaches 0.80, and otherwise primes. Without the model there are no intent reflexes, and nothing fails.

          In the code: crates/adage-learn/src/intent.rs · crates/adage-intent

          04

          Graph engine: repeated subgraphs, chosen by compression engine flag

          The graph engine generalises the five miners. Events are embedded and clustered into prototype labels, so each trace becomes a string, and repeated subgraphs become repeated substrings. Mining starts from a step worth delivering and grows closed patterns around it. A pattern is read as a prediction, after this context, this follows, with s supporting traces out of n opportunities. Its confidence is shrunk toward its parent pattern's confidence m:

          θ∼Beta(α,β),α=s+κm,β=(n−s)+κ(1−m),κ=2

          Counts are discounted with a 60-day half-life, and a candidate is judged by the posterior's 5% quantile, q0.05. Calibration then compares posterior means with held-out outcomes on 3 task-grouped folds, using isotonic regression over 200 bins.

          Frequent is not the same as useful, so selection is by minimum description length: a reflex earns its place by describing the traces in fewer bits.

          L(D,CT)=L(CT)+L(D∣CT),L(X∣CT)=−log2u(X)∑Yu(Y)

          A pattern in the code table costs its items' codes, plus 4 bits per item and log2 6 bits for its length. Tables fill best gain first, re-checking each gain before accepting it. Candidates that would deliver different directives never compete: each delivery shape gets its own table, and dead ends one per failing runner. Winners compile to delivery shapes, and a sequence starts provisional (prime) until its evidence is counted again on its own trigger. An interlock drops any reflex that would direct a runner a dead end says fails. On the core suite the graph engine scores J 0.9759, against 0.6307 for the miners.

          Design: docs/tracks/track-g-graph-engine.md · crates/adage-graph

          05

          The index: one sealed file

          Every installed track compiles into one list. The same behaviour gets the same id wherever it was found, so duplicates collide, and precedence decides which copy stays: your own evidence, then a teammate's signed pack, then an instinct. The survivors are serialised in order and hashed, and the hook reads only that file:

          generation=hex(SHA-256(index))[0:16)

          Tracks are versioned: evidence moving bumps the patch number, an added reflex the minor, a changed or removed one the major. Imported reflexes start forming and never fire until your own traces confirm them. Instincts always deliver as prime and retire themselves if they keep misfiring.

          In the code: crates/adage-tracks/src/{index,interlock}.rs · adage-instincts · adage-pack

          06

          The hook: a pure decision, and a control arm

          Each hook event runs a pure function of the event and the index. Before anything is said, the session is assigned to an arm by a hash, so the assignment is stable and reproducible:

          x=u32le(SHA-256("adage-arm:"∥session)[0:4))232−1,control⇔x<0.10

          A control session runs the same decision, records what it would have said, and stays silent. Each record carries its propensity, the probability of the arm it got.

          Slots
          a value from the prompt binds only if it matches the contract learned from real values and is a plain token: at most 200 characters from A–Z a–z 0–9 _ . / : @ = + , -, with no leading dash
          Followed
          a directive stays pending for 3 tool batches; it is followed if a batch runs its canonical command (options may be added), and ignored otherwise
          Fails open
          if the hook errors or runs out of time, the agent carries on as if Adage were not installed

          In the code: crates/adage-runtime/src/lib.rs (assign_arm) · crates/adage-hook

          07

          Measurement: the task is the unit

          Runs of one task are not independent, so the task, not the run, is the unit. For each of J tasks, compare the arms' means, then average over tasks:

          dj=x¯jadage−x¯jcontrol,T=1J∑j=1Jdj

          No distribution is assumed. Arm labels are shuffled within each task, never across tasks, 10,000 times, and the p-value counts how often chance does at least as well:

          p=1+#{b:|Tb*|≥|T|}1+N,N=10,000

          Whether the effect would hold for new tasks is a separate question. A sign-flip test over the task differences answers it exactly up to 16 tasks, where its smallest possible p is 2/2J:

          pflip=#{s∈{±1}J:|∑jsjdj|≥|∑jdj|}2J

          Turns, cost and pass rate are tested together, so Holm's step-down corrects for looking at several metrics. With the m p-values sorted ascending:

          p~(i)=maxk≤imin(1,(m−k+1)p(k))
          Interval
          a 95% percentile bootstrap that resamples whole tasks 2,000 times
          Per reflex
          a share is stated only with at least 10 treated and 5 control traces
          Offline bench
          before any agent runs, a learner scores J=turns saved−2.0×false directives+1.0×route F1 per session

          In the code: demo/report.py (cluster_perm_p, task_signflip_p, cluster_boot_ci, holm) · crates/adage-eval · evals/live

          08

          Training on a skill: claims get verdicts

          A SKILL.md, Makefile or CI file becomes typed claims, each a hypothesis to test. Adage generates tasks that give each claim its chance, plus controls that touch nothing it covers, and runs your own agent headlessly on each in a sealed clone with no remote. The traces go through the same miners, and reflexes are staged at prime, never activated. Held-out tasks then run in three arms: no skill, the skill in the prompt, and the staged reflexes through the hooks. A reflex is accepted when:

          freflex≥0.70,fskill−freflex≤0.10,creflexcskill≤1.10

          Here f is the share of sessions that followed the step and c the cost in turns or dollars. A claim the agents contradict, like a test command that fails where another works, comes back as a dead end, and the skill is told which of its claims are stale.

          In the code: crates/adage-train · docs/training.md
